# What goes in each part

## Part 1: the Show Map (for the creator)
Keep this order and these section names, and change the content to fit the app.

- **A. What you need, and what it costs:**
  - the computer, an AI app that can build on your computer (the alphabetical list of several apps), and the disk
    space;
  - a table of each part of the app, what runs it, and its cost;
  - a score row: messages the first time, messages this way, messages in speed mode, and the cost ($0).
- **B. Your first 10 minutes:** install your AI app, open the part of it that can work on your computer, make an
  empty folder, and allow permissions. Use the per-app setup table, and never name just one app.
- **C. What we learned the first time:** a table of what happened, what it cost, and the fix that's now in the
  kickoff. Then one line on what worked and should stay.
- **Step 0. Turn on the prompt log:** a copy box with one line asking the AI to keep a log, for example: "Keep a
  prompt log: after each of my messages, add it and one line on what you did to PROMPTS.md in this folder."
- **Step 1. The kickoff message:** a copy box, with [brackets] for the reader's choices, then a sample AI reply.
- **Steps 2 to N. The build:** for each step:
  - a "You" block ("Works. Next step.");
  - a "You test" block: what to try, and what looks great on camera;
  - Tips.
  Add an "if something's off" message wherever a real bug happened.
- **Step N+1. Wrap-up:** a one-page guide, and the prompt log tidied up to share.
- **D. The fastest version:** a table of about four messages that builds everything.
- **E. Phrases that save time:** a table of "say this", "instead of" and "why".
- **F. Words you'll hear:** a short glossary.
- **G. Presenter notes (off camera):**
  - pause your own copy of the app while filming;
  - use a fresh folder and a fresh app name;
  - what to have ready;
  - a reminder to point viewers to Part 2;
  - the "I'm using ..., but any AI coding app works" line (see `rules.md`);
  - the opener line from the `app-opener-video` skill, if you made an opener.

Part 1 uses the same color system as the kit and reuses the kit's screenshots in its steps.

## Part 2: the build-it-yourself kit (for viewers and customers)
It starts on a new page with its own title and contents, and must make sense to someone who never saw the show:

1. **What you'll end up with:** the features, in plain words, with a screenshot of the finished app.
2. **What you need:** the computer, an AI app that can build on your computer, the space, and the cost table.
3. **Get set up (10 minutes):** with the per-app table.
4. **Choose your options:** a table of each choice, with a suggested answer and a note (name, keys, style, words,
   which extras).
5. **The one message that builds it:** the kickoff without the prompt log line, plus the "all in one go" variant.
6. **Try each step:** a table of each step and what to try, with one screenshot per step. Explain the "Works. Next
   step." habit.
7. **Make it your own:** a table of "you want" against "say to the AI": keys, writing style, per-app styles, new
   commands, sounds, fewer features, look, where things are saved.
8. **When something goes wrong:** a table of what happens against what to say to the AI, built from the real bugs.
9. **Stay safe:** drafts only, consent, privacy, backups, trusted downloads, sites' rules.
10. **Give it to a friend:** a copy-box prompt for a clean, offline friend version with one-click setup, tested in
    Windows Sandbox (a throwaway, clean copy of Windows) before sharing. Leave this out if the app can't be shared.
11. **Words you'll hear.**

## Colorful, with real screenshots
Kits that people follow along with should be bright, friendly and full of pictures.

- **Color:** each section gets its own soft accent color: a colored section number or band, tinted callout boxes for
  tips and warnings, and colored table headers. The copy boxes look like a chat message. Keep the text easy to read
  (contrast at least 4.5 to 1), and make sure it prints well in color.
- **Screenshots:** real pictures of the app, never mock-ups:
  - the finished app at the top, in "What you'll end up with";
  - one per step in "Try each step", showing what the reader should see;
  - one for each of the main settings or choices.
- Take them at 100% or 125% scale, in light mode unless dark tells the story better.
- Every picture uses made-up test data only: no real names, photos, emails, or file paths that show a person's name.
- Each picture has a one-line caption.
- Embed the pictures in the HTML (as data URIs, or as files next to the page) so the printed PDF carries them.
