# Rules for both parts

## Write for beginners who have only used AI chat
- Plain words, short sentences, and every term explained once.
- No code to write. Readers paste messages, try what the AI builds, and say what happened.

## Say "AI", and never single out one brand
The reader can use whatever AI they like, so the text doesn't steer them.
- Write "AI" or "the AI": "build your own voice app with AI", "the AI checks your computer", "say to the AI", "a
  message you type to the AI". The speaker label on sample replies is "AI".
- Describe setup generically: "an AI app that can build on your computer: one that can create files and run
  programs for you, not just chat", and "a paid plan for it, if it needs one". Then "open the part of your AI app
  that can work on your computer", and "connect Gmail or Outlook in your AI app's settings".
- Costs say "$0 beyond your AI plan".

## Any AI coding app works; say so, and never steer to one
Name several AI coding apps side by side, in alphabetical order, and never just one: "Antigravity, Claude Code,
Codex or Cursor, or another". Names appear in only three places:
- **What you need:** add one line, "Any of them works; nothing here is tied to one."
- **Get set up:** a per-app table saying where to open your folder and paste the message, and how each app asks for
  permission.
- **The glossary:** an "AI coding app" entry.

Everywhere else the text says "the AI". Show Map sections A and B get the same line.

## Say what you built it with
Readers like to know what the creator used, as long as it's clear they can use something else.
- **Kit cover:** right under the quick facts, a colored key box labeled "Made with `<your AI app>`, works with any AI
  app". Its text: "We built ours with `<your AI app>`. You can use any AI coding app you like, for example
  `<two or three others, alphabetical>`: the messages and steps are the same. Section 3 shows where to paste the
  message in each."
- **Public README** (if the app goes in a public repository): the same note right under the intro, in a GitHub
  `> [!NOTE]` box.
- **Show Map G (presenter notes):** a line to say on camera: "I'm using `<your AI app>`, but any AI coding app works
  the same way: `<the others, alphabetical>`, whatever you like."

## Public repository copies
If the app goes in a public repository, its folder gets an `AGENTS.md` with build and test notes that any AI coding
app reads. `CLAUDE.md` and `GEMINI.md` just point to it.

## Free models on the reader's own computer
- No paid services, API keys or cloud accounts.
- If the creator's own copy uses a paid service, leave that out.
- If a feature can't work without something paid, mark it "Optional" and say what it needs.

## Windows PC by default
Cover the Mac or Linux only if the creator asks.

## Fewest prompts
- One kickoff message settles every decision, and puts each lesson from the first build in as a line.
- After that, the reader mostly says "Works. Next step."
- Also give a "fastest version" of about four messages that builds everything without stopping.

## Real numbers from the real build
- How many messages the first build took, and how many the script takes.
- The costs (normally $0 beyond the reader's AI plan).
- The download sizes.
- The timings.

## Safety lines stay in every kickoff
- Drafts only: never send or delete.
- Tests never screenshot the real screen, press real keys, change the volume or send anything.
- Big steps are built in a copy and swapped in when the tests pass.
- Nothing ever fails silently.

Add a consent line for anything that records other people, and "captions first" for anything that takes content from
a site.

## Nothing private
No email addresses, account names, keys, tenant or account IDs, personal folder paths, or the creator's own words or
style files. Screenshots use made-up test data only.

## American English
Spell it the American way everywhere ("color", "favor", "organize", "resume" with no accents). Keep contractions.
